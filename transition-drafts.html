Page transition drafts

Three ways to move between pages. Each one covers the screen on the way out, then holds on the new page until its content is actually ready — so you never land on a black box waiting for photos. Press play to feel the motion; each demo fakes a 1.4 second load so you can see the hold.

Draft A

Cream curtain

A cream panel sweeps up over the page and the DAKE VISUALS wordmark drifts in behind it. On arrival the panel keeps travelling upward and off. Same language as your homepage intro, so the whole site moves one way.

Draft B

Blue iris

A circle of your blue opens from the link you clicked until it fills the screen, then contracts away on the new page. Ties navigation to the ink-pool effect on the homepage CTA — one idea, used twice.

Draft C

Shutter

Five cream columns drop in one after another to close over the page, then lift in sequence to reveal the next. Reads like a camera shutter or a film strip — the most "studio" of the three.

Draft D

Letterbox

Two black bars close in from top and bottom until they meet, the way a film frame crops to scope. They part again on the new page. Quiet, cinematic, and the only dark option here.

Draft E

Rack focus

The page you are leaving drifts out of focus and washes to cream, then the next one pulls back into focus. Borrows the language of a lens hunting for focus — the most filmic of the six.

Draft F

Named panel

A blue panel sweeps across carrying the name of where you are headed in huge type, then keeps going and drops you there. The only one that tells the visitor what is coming.

All three use the same mechanic underneath: cover → navigate → wait for the new page's real content → reveal. On BTS that means waiting for the photos to decode, on About for the video's first frame. A safety cap lifts the cover anyway if something is slow, so a stalled asset can never trap you behind it.
